01 / The deal is broken

You came for the crafts.
Instagram got in the way.

#

Hashtags got gutted.

A shared tag can hold a whole community: a knitalong, a sewing challenge, a tiny specialist craft. Instagram removed hashtag following in December 2024, then announced a five-hashtag caption limit in December 2025. Tags still exist. Following a community through one no longer does.

[3] [4]

CraftSky: searchable hashtags alongside craft, pattern and material information. Find a project through the details its maker shared.

?

Where’s that pattern?
Good luck.

A wall of recommendations is a lousy substitute for a craft library. When you want to see how a pattern looks on someone’s finished project, popularity isn’t the point. Instagram ranks recommendations by predicted interests and engagement. CraftSky gives project details a place of their own.

[2]

CraftSky: projects with structured details and search filters. Less detective work. More inspiration.

↯

They followed you.
The feed has other plans.

Following someone should mean seeing their work. Instagram’s main feed mixes followed accounts with suggested posts and ads. A follow is no guarantee of a place in that feed. The chronological Following feed is tucked away in a menu, exactly where Instagram chooses to keep it.

[1] [14]

CraftSky: people you follow, newest first, in your home feed. Search and discovery help you find someone new without taking over that feed.

02 / The real cost of “free”

You’re holding the phone.
Who’s pulling the thread?

Instagram sells ads while you scroll.

In 2020, Instagram added suggested posts after the end of your followed-account feed. Once you had caught up with the people you followed, it gave you more recommendations to keep you scrolling.

[2]

CraftSky has no ads to sell. Our aim: help you catch up, get inspired, then get back to your craft.

Inspiration shouldn’t leave you feeling small.

Comparison. Pressure to perform. One more scroll instead of one more row. Research links heavy social-media use with mental-health risks, and edited Instagram photos with poorer body image in teenage girls.

[9] [15]

We choose a chronological home feed and no advertising. Your worth was never a view count.

Your creativity. Their AI.

Meta announced training on public adult posts and comments in the EU in 2025, with a right to object there. It says private messages with friends and family are excluded unless someone shares them with its AI. Public does not mean you expected your work to become training material.

[6]

CraftSky will not use your content to train generative AI. Your creativity belongs to you.

[12]

The sales pitch learns from you.

Meta says activity shapes ad recommendations. It announced that interactions with its AI would also inform content and ads from December 2025 in most regions. A conversation about an interest can become another commercial signal.

[7]

CraftSky’s commitment: no ads, no sale or rental of personal information, no sharing for cross-service behavioural advertising.

[13]

03 / A pattern worth recognising

They keep changing the deal.

Instagram has cut discovery tools while finding new ways to charge creators and use their work. Here’s how the deal has changed.

  1. 2020

    The end of the feed stopped being the end.

    Suggested posts gave you more recommendations to scroll through after you had seen posts from the people you followed.

    [2]
  2. 2022

    More recommendations were the plan.

    Meta introduced Following and Favourites while explicitly saying it would add more recommendations to the main feed.

    [1]
  3. 2023

    A subscription for the blue badge.

    Meta Verified bundled paid verification, protection and access to account support. Another bill for creators building on somebody else’s platform.

    [5]
  4. 2024

    Hashtag following got the axe.

    The ability to follow a shared interest through a hashtag was removed. Communities lost a useful route into the feed.

    [3]
  5. 2025

    Public posts became AI material in the EU.

    Meta announced training using adults’ public content, with an objection mechanism for EU users.

    [6]
  6. 2025

    Five tags. More commercial signals.

    December brought the announced five-hashtag cap and the start date for using AI interactions in recommendations and ads in most regions. These were separate changes, affecting both what creators can post and how Meta uses their activity.

    [4] [7]
  7. 2026and beyond

    What will they change next?

    How many more ads will come between you and the people you follow? Which useful discovery tool could disappear next? What new uses of your creative work will you be asked to accept?

    And if the next change hurts your community, how much say will you actually have?

    You shouldn’t have to wait for the next bad deal to look for a better home.

05 / Leaving shouldn’t mean starting over

A download isn’t
an exit.

Instagram lets you download your information. It doesn’t let you take that account and its connections to a competing service. After years of building a community, leaving still means asking everyone to find you somewhere else. CraftSky uses the open AT Protocol to keep your account separate from the app.

Our commitments

What we won’t do.

  • Sell advertising in your feed.
  • Sell or rent your personal information.
  • Reorder your home feed to maximise engagement.
  • Use your content to train generative AI.

These are our commitments. The network gives you choices; it doesn’t make those promises for us.

[13] [12]

The structural difference

Your account isn’t ours.

Your public posts, profile and follows are stored with your account, on a server separate from CraftSky. The protocol lets you move that hosting while keeping your identity and connections.

Compatible apps can use those records. CraftSky can moderate its own service; it doesn’t control the whole network.

[10]

Open source · AGPL-3.0

You can check the work.

You can’t take Instagram’s complete app and service code and build your own version. CraftSky’s code is public.

Developers can inspect how it works, contribute improvements, or build another version under the licence. The code can outlast the company.
